### General Information:

In the United States, voting laws are established at both the federal and state levels. Federal laws protect voting rights and establish certain requirements for elections, while individual states are responsible for managing most election procedures. This means that voter registration deadlines, identification requirements, early voting, and absentee voting rules can vary depending on where someone lives.

To vote in elections, people generally must be U.S. citizens, meet their state's residency requirements, be at least 18 years old, and meet their state's voter registration and eligibility rules. Most Americans currently vote in person, through early voting, or by submitting absentee or mail-in ballots.

Although technology is widely used in elections, most states do not allow voters to submit completed ballots through the internet. Concerns about cybersecurity, voter privacy, and election integrity have limited the adoption of online voting. However, some states have introduced electronic ballot return options for certain groups, including military personnel, overseas voters, and individuals with disabilities.
